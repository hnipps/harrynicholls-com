gitrove

Daily discoveries from your GitHub network.

gitrove watches the developers you follow and the repositories you star. Once a day, it sends a digest of the discoveries, releases, and connections worth a closer look.

Built with Go, PostgreSQL, and a server-rendered TypeScript frontend.

Three ways to arrive

The star, project, and words stay the same. What changes is how the place opens up around you.

Beside the star

You stop alongside gitrove. The star holds its place on the left; a readable project page sits beside it. You keep the strongest sense of where you are.

Strongest at orientation. It gives up some reading width to keep the star and its surroundings present.

The reading space

The approach brings gitrove close, and its region opens into a broad reading space. The galaxy remains at the edges while the project gets your full attention.

Strongest for reading case studies. The destination feels more like a place you enter, with less visible space around it.

Around the star

The star becomes the centre of a small exhibition. A title, a real interface, and a description occupy different places around it. You explore one project at a local scale.

Strongest as a spatial experience. On a phone its separate pieces need an authored reading order.

The journey

Two scales. Work, About, Writing, and Contact are regions. Work resolves into six individual project stars as you approach.

A stable geography. The same star stays in the same place. A direct navigation link and its star take you to the same destination.

Travel, then read. Motion carries the transition. At the destination the camera rests, text faces you, and the content has room to be read.

The next technical step. This storyboard uses Canvas 2D and real HTML overlays. Once the arrival is chosen, a separate feasibility study can put the labels and reading surfaces into HTML-in-Canvas, checking interaction, geometry, selection, and fallback behaviour.
