# Matte flexible paper

Mode: Experience. The user explicitly requested visible flexibility in the selected sheet, and then removal of the glossy look. This continues the approved Tactile paper direction. No texture, palette, copy or navigation redesign was requested.

The earlier implementation rotated a single rigid rectangle and curved only its outline. That cannot deform content across the surface. It has been replaced by a connected 64 × 16 mesh. Tangent angles vary across each row; integration preserves strip length as the sheet bends. A travelling turn, cross-sheet twist and gradual flattening mean the sheet's parts no longer share one orientation. The reverse pattern and captured front content follow the surface. Unit checks establish more than 200px of midpoint deviation from a flat chord during the turn, simultaneous front/back orientations, length preservation and exact landing coordinates.

The original iframe stays alive and is temporarily hidden during the 1650ms flight. A same-origin viewport picture is used only for the mesh, with visible images embedded, computed custom properties preserved, scripts removed and animation frozen. The snapshot includes the selected document's scroll position. On landing the mesh and GPU resources are removed and the untouched live document is shown. Transparent document backgrounds are filled before upload, so the native HTML theme remains white. A matte DOM turn remains available if capture or WebGL cannot initialize. Reduced motion bypasses capture and mesh creation. Resizing or enabling reduced motion completes an ongoing turn immediately.

Gloss removal: fan-face highlight overlays and bright inset edges were deleted. Front/back specular sweep overlays were deleted. The corner gradient now uses the existing paper ground with a muted crease tone rather than a bright white highlight. The mesh uses diffuse orientation shading only; no animated blur, glossy pass, displacement texture or extra moving light layer is rendered.

Verification used one batched desktop/phone inspection followed by one correction and confirmation. The first mesh path kept too much of the turn offscreen. Accelerating its center movement independently of the rotation keeps the paper visible while its bend develops. Final captures show the reverse rolling inward, the front content wrapped around the surface, and gradual flattening. Files: `.taste-skill/flexible-paper/final/`.

Checks: thirteen geometry/history/flex tests pass. Real-browser mesh checks pass on desktop and phone, including source-pose alignment, five flight poses, cleanup, live-page restoration and corner response. The sheaf suite passes mouse, touch, keyboard, state preservation, history and reset. The hover suite retains all five exposed 44px targets at 1440/768/390px with stationary pointer stability. All six themes produce opaque viewport textures. A forced no-WebGL check successfully uses the fallback and selects the requested view. No browser runtime exceptions were observed. The detector reports no findings with the existing narrowly scoped exceptions. Source lint reports zero failures/warnings. Original-study and production preservation hashes match.

Performance scope: one front picture and small reverse tile are uploaded once; the GPU buffer is allocated once and updated for the connected mesh. Rendering is capped at 1.5 device pixels per CSS pixel and 2048 pixels on either axis. The canvas exists only for the transition. Functional and visual verification used Chrome on this development machine with touch emulation; physical-device frame rate and other engines were not measured. Removing glossy layers is verified; a speedup relative to the previous version has not been claimed.

No new dependencies or remote rendering services were added. Nothing was deployed.
