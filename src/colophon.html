Style guide

One column, a serif for the name and a sans for the reading, one blue and one red. Any other colour belongs to the album covers.

Inks

Paper and ink share one hue of blue. The accent is a red a third of the way round the colour wheel. Both palettes were tuned in OKLCH, where equal steps in lightness look equal.

Rules

  • One accent. The red marks things: underlines, bullets, today’s dot. It never fills a shape or sets a paragraph.
  • The serif is the name. Everything else is the sans, in two weights.
  • Upright for the record, italic for anything said in passing.
  • No cards, no boxes. Sections are a hairline and a heading.

Grid

A wide screen has two left edges and no more. The page edge carries the name, the hairlines and headings like the ones beside this text. The text edge carries everything read, in a column about seventy characters wide.

Interaction

Links are underlined in the accent, and the underline thickens under the pointer. Try this one. On the home page, the dot on the “i” of my name switches between light and dark. Album titles stay out of the way until you point at a cover.

Data

A year of commits

The graph is my public GitHub contribution calendar, fetched when the site deploys. Each dot is a day. Its size follows the square root of that day’s count, capped so a few huge days do not flatten the rest. If GitHub cannot be reached, the last good graph stays up.

Albums

Chosen by hand and kept in a short list. Cover art and links come from Apple’s catalogue.
